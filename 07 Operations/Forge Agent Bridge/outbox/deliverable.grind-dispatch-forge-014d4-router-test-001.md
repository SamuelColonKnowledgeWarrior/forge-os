## 3. Positioning Angle (draft — Tier 2 approval required before public use)

Position the book not as a compliance manual but as a **leadership credibility tool**: the frontline supervisor who runs a visibly safe crew is the one who gets promoted, keeps their best people, and avoids the incidents that end careers. This reframes "safety" from a checkbox obligation to a leadership differentiator — broadening appeal beyond EHS specialists to ambitious operations leaders.

Three candidate headline directions (pick one, or A/B test):
1. **"Safety is a leadership skill, not a checklist."** — appeals to career-driven supervisors.
2. **"The crew that trusts you is the crew that stays."** — appeals to retention-focused ops managers.
3. **"Every incident report is a leadership report."** — appeals to EHS/L&D buyers evaluating training material.

## 5. Channel Plan (Tier 1 — architecture only; spend requires Tier 3 approval)

1. **Owned/email reactivation** — if a prior list exists, this is the lowest-cost, highest-intent channel; sequence a 3-part relaunch series (why now / what's inside / how to buy or bulk-order).
2. **LinkedIn organic + targeted content** — short-form posts built around the positioning angle (Section 3), aimed at supervisors and EHS leaders; no paid spend without Tier 3 sign-off.
3. **Trade/industry publication outreach** — pitch a bylined article or excerpt to relevant safety/operations trade press; earned media, no spend required.
4. **Bulk/B2B landing page** — dedicated page for EHS/L&D buyers purchasing for teams, since this segment has a materially different buying process than individual readers.
5. **Author-led webinar or panel** — positions the book as a leadership resource rather than a product; requires author availability (unconfirmed).

Paid channels (LinkedIn ads, trade publication placements, retargeting) are flagged as options but **not recommended for commitment** until baseline audience/list data is confirmed and Tier 3 approves spend.
